I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Agricultural and Applied Economics at the University of Wisconsin-Madison, advised by Dustin Frye. My fields are development, environment and political economy.

My current work studies how the leadership of large organizations shapes long-run outcomes in the communities they serve, and how land ownership patterns affect wildfire risk and insurance markets. Much of it relies on spatial data and newly digitized historical sources.

Before Madison, I was a pre-doctoral fellow at the Energy Policy Institute at the University of Chicago, where I also received a B.A. in Economics and a B.A. in Statistics.

Research

Work in progress

From Missionaries to Bishops: Localized Church Leadership and Family Life in Francophone Africa

Across the former French Africa, Catholic jurisdictions passed from European-born to African-born bishops at staggered dates over 6 decades. Using the Church's own jurisdiction maps, archival episcopal records and pooled demographic surveys, I study the long-run consequences of replacing foreign with indigenous leaders in a global hierarchical organization, holding the organization's presence fixed.

Parcel Fragmentation and the Wildfire Insurance Spiral: Evidence from California

Wildfire mitigation on private land protects neighbors as well as owners, so fragmented ownership invites free-riding. This project asks whether parcel fragmentation amplifies the cycle of low mitigation, rising premiums and retreat to the residual insurance market, and whether community programs and insurance reforms dampen it.
